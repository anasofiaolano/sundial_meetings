### Attendees

### Key Topics Covered

**Day-to-Day Workflow**
- Cell phone connected to work phone; reviews incoming texts, emails, and voicemails before arriving at the office each morning.
- Starts each day clearing emails and voicemails, prioritizing by CRM notes and perceived lead temperature — no system-driven prioritization.
- Phone rings constantly with both sales and non-sales calls; Lucas acts as a de facto receptionist, transferring a high percentage of calls to Log Home Mart or the builder hotline.
- Phone provider was switched approximately a year ago; Lucas suspects some builder/partner contacts may have outdated extensions, contributing to misdirected call volume.

**Lead Management**
- Approximately 100 active contacts in his pipeline, managed via CRM aging report and self-set reminders.
- Roughly 25% are "hot" — touched weekly. Others are touched monthly or quarterly depending on readiness. Those without property yet are touched on a ~3-month cycle.
- Mental tracking is the primary load-bearing system; described as a stress and energy drain.
- Uses new YouTube video releases as a soft-touch outreach for mid-funnel prospects ("not pressing for anything, just keeping your name in front of them").
- Does not use email templates — writes all outreach manually. Acknowledged that templates would be helpful but hasn't had time to build them.
- Does not help prospects find land; response is essentially "call me when you have land," though the team is building a professional referral database of local realtors.

**Sales Process Milestones**
1. Qualify prospect; surface realistic budget (often results in sticker shock — managing this is central to conversion).
2. Issue formal ballpark.
3. Collect property/job-specific details; get signed design service agreement with payment.
4. Bring client in to choose product and finalize plans.
- Primary stall point before DSA: proving value vs. conventional construction cost. Competitor names are rarely disclosed by prospects; Lucas believes "shopping around" is often a bluff given Golden Eagle's more inclusive offering.
- A small number of buyers (~1–2/year) purchase materials only (DIY path).

**Team Coordination**
- No formal weekly pipeline review or content calendar. Sales meetings are ad hoc, roughly quarterly, run by Chris Stitcher. At the sales meeting held the day of this interview, the team received a preview of Juan's new CRM system; Lucas said he is excited to see it in its completed form.
- Jay (head of marketing) informally notifies the sales team of upcoming content drops or boosted posts.
- No shared email templates or playbooks across the sales team.

**Pain Points**
- Mental load of tracking 100 contacts without systematic support.
- Acting as receptionist for non-sales calls.
- Writing all emails from scratch.
- No automated follow-up prompts or task generation from call notes.

**What Lucas Wants**
- Predetermined/templated email responses that can be quickly personalized.
- Call transcription + automatic CRM note entry + auto-generated tasks and reminders from conversation content. This is his top-priority wish: "that makes my gray hair turn brown again."
- Less mental load so he can handle more calls and follow-ups.
- Willing to provide a list of FAQs and common email types to assist template-building.

**AI Familiarity**
- Has used Copilot and ChatGPT for basic tasks (drafting, editing, inspiration).
- Self-describes as AI-illiterate; doesn't know what's available beyond what he's stumbled into.
- Interested in next week's AI training; prefers a hands-on, practical format.

## Jeff Parmeter — Sales Discovery Interview

### Attendees
- Jeff Parmeter (Client Advisor / Sales)
- Daniel
- Anna

### Key Topics Covered

**Background & Role**
- Career background in insurance adjusting, insurance supervision, and auto body repair — not log/home building.
- Started at Golden Eagle in June of last year; brought over by Jay for his communication and people skills.
- Has prior experience helping define requirements for claims management software (including integration with estimating platforms) — gives him a practical mental model of CRM systems.
- Self-describes as having a "high level or basic understanding" of the CRM/CTM.

**Day-to-Day Workflow**
- Starts each morning reviewing CTM reminders — considers this his primary organizational system, more so than other reps.
- Uses reminders to track: pricing status, floor plan design status, and follow-up timing for leads.
- After reminders, moves to email: reads and responds to client/lead messages, then copies and pastes email content into CTM notes for full documentation.
- Phone calls are documented as summaries (not verbatim) due to the nature of verbal conversation.
- Texts are copied and pasted in full into the CTM note section with a label (e.g., "text string from Jeff and Jim Bob") using the Elevate phone system's SMS capability.
- Strong documentation philosophy from his insurance background: every touchpoint — call, email, text — should be noted.

**Lead Handling & Follow-Up Cadence**
- Leads come in via phone or email; it's first-come-first-served across the sales team (whoever picks up first gets the lead).
- Jeff's standard cadence after initial contact: **1 week → 30 days → 3 months**.
- Immediately establishes preferred communication method (phone, text, email) and uses that method consistently.
- Outliers modify the cadence: if a prospect says "follow up when I close on my property in two weeks," Jeff sets the reminder accordingly.
- Will disqualify leads that show no meaningful response rather than continuing to reach out indefinitely.
- Noted that each rep has their own playbook — no enforced team-wide sequence.

**All CTM Actions Are Manual**
- Confirmed: everything in the CTM is done manually. No automated tasks — unless a rep sets them.
- Jeff sets his own reminders for every follow-up (1 week, 30 days, 3 months, or custom).

**Relationship-Building Approach**
- Believes clients "buy me before they buy the product."
- Uses probing questions to surface personal context: what's driving the decision, where they want to build, personal interests.
- Notes personal details (hobbies, family, motivations) in the CTM's personal/quick-info notes section — uses them as rapport hooks in future calls.
- Views open, proactive communication as a core principle — always updating clients on status, even when news is slow.

**Sales Process**
- Primary milestone: get the prospect to sign the **Design Service Agreement (DSA)** and pay the design deposit.
- Fee: **$4/design sq ft** for homes up to 10,000 sq ft. Design sq ft includes covered decks, porches, garages, finished lower level, etc. — often much larger than the base footprint. Example: 2,000 sq ft home → ~6,700 design sq ft → ~$25,000 deposit.
- Deposit is credited back at full contract signing. Functions as a commitment filter to weed out non-serious prospects and protect engineering/design cycle time.
- After DSA: engineering begins floor plans and elevations. Client receives a Google Doc survey about how they want to live in the home (interior vision, color palettes, gatherings, etc.) — feeds the interior design group.
- DocuSign used for some agreements; accounting manually enters the signed information into the CTM.

**Pipeline & Conversion**
- Estimated conversion rate: **~2–3%** (lead to full contract). Jeff described this as conservative.
- Inbound volume is content-driven: on a day a new home tour drops → **10–20 calls**; slower days with no new content → **~5 new lead calls**.

**Pain Points**
- All reminders and follow-up tasks are manually set — no automation.
- Texting requires manual copy-paste into CTM; no native SMS logging.
- Documentation of phone calls is summary-only — no verbatim record.
- Current reminder workflow is "busy work" — meaningful but time-consuming.

**What Jeff Wants**
- **Automated reminders and outreach prompts** — top priority. A system that automatically reaches out to clients or prompts Jeff to do so based on status (e.g., "Jeff spoke to you a week ago, here's what's next").
- **Non-response tracking with disqualification prompts**: e.g., after 6 attempts with no response from a prospect, the system asks "do you want to maintain this relationship or disqualify?"
- Described this as the "low hanging fruit" for automation.

**AI Familiarity**
- Not explicitly discussed; implied comfort with technology through his claims management software background.

### Key Topics Covered

**Company Evolution (John's Perspective)**
- Over the last ~10 years, the biggest change has been the caliber of homes and clientele.
- Early on: smaller cabin-type homes primarily in the Midwest.
- Now: expanded geographically across the country; homes are roughly double the square footage; higher level of custom finish.
- Social media and YouTube home tours are naturally gravitating the customer base toward a higher-end, higher-expectation clientele — people want to buy what they see.

**Day-to-Day Workflow**
- Roughly **40% of time** on active plan-service or actively-building clients.
- Remaining ~60% split across: incoming emails, new phone calls, aging report follow-ups.
- Manages approximately **10–15 active clients** (plan service or active build) at any given time.
- Maintains a pool of approximately **100 longer-horizon contacts** in the CRM, followed up via aging report.
- Acknowledged there is a cap on how many active clients can be managed well — beyond that cap, client attention suffers and deals can be lost.
- Uses Zoom for floor plan review sessions with clients; calls post-meeting sketch updates "redlines," which are then submitted to the design team.

**Lead Handling**
- Inbound calls arrive raw — no pre-submitted data since the CRM web form was disabled.
- Initial call goal: qualify on budget, location, time frame, home preferences. Determine whether to enter the prospect into the CRM at all.
- The **two-year mark** is the explicit qualification threshold: prospects planning to build within two years are engaged actively and moved toward a ballpark; those further out are tracked in the longer-horizon pool.
- Follow-up is tailored to each client's specific situation (e.g., "did you find land?" vs. "here's a new YouTube video").

**CRM Usage**
- Calls are not recorded; notes are taken manually and entered into the CRM.
- **No file/attachment support**: the home-built CRM cannot store attachments. Email attachments must be retrieved from the email inbox separately — cross-referencing is a named pain point.
- For active clients, relies primarily on memory. For longer-horizon contacts, re-reads CRM notes before reaching out to personalize the touchpoint.

**Phone & Communication Systems**
- Uses **Elevate** phone app on personal cell — calls and texts go through the office number. Described migration as smooth.
- Key benefit of Elevate: no longer required to be in the office on Saturdays.
- Active communication channels: phone (Elevate), email. Web lead form is currently disabled.

**Quota & Sales Culture**
- Senior Sales Advisors are expected to hit **$4.7 million** in sold invoices (top-line, not profit).
- Sales culture: collaborative internally, competitive on inbound grabs. Reps help each other with deal strategy and builder referrals; no cutthroat dynamic.
- Sales meetings are approximately **quarterly**; John sees value in more frequent structured sessions.

**AI & Technology Familiarity**
- Self-described as "not real super techy."
- Uses **ChatGPT** for: cleaning up client email responses, and generating/elaborating blog content for the website.
- Supportive of call recording + automatic note entry — would save time re-entering notes and improve accuracy vs. recalling a 90-minute conversation from memory.
- No strong current pain points volunteered; open to seeing what's possible once the concept is more concrete.

**Next Steps Communicated to John**

**Follow-Up Cadence & CRM Usage**
- After initial call, enters prospect info into the in-house CRM: name, building location, address, email, topics discussed.

- Leads appear on each rep's home screen tagged as hot, medium, or disqualified.
- A daily checklist/notification pops up based on manually set reminders (similar to a meeting notification).

**Sales Process Observations**
- Every home is deeply custom — no standard price list, no unmodified off-the-shelf floor plan.
- Sales relationship with a client lasts approximately **1.5 years on average**; for very large custom homes (e.g., 17,000 sq ft), the relationship can span **2–3 years**.
- The sales team's involvement does not end until the client's last load of materials arrives — typically a couple of months before the home is complete.
- Deep, educational relationships with prospects are necessary given the product's complexity and cost.
- Ballpark pricing is provided early, described to the client as within approximately **10% accuracy**, intended to self-qualify or disqualify before investing further in design/engineering.
- After ballpark acceptance: **architectural design plan** — priced by the square foot, functions as a deposit credited toward the final home price. Initiates design and engineering work.
- Floor plan iteration with the client happens approximately **5–6 times** before moving to a materials proposal (hard price).
- Detailed materials pricing (down to individual two-by-fours) is handled by a dedicated pricing department.

- Many separate standalone forms covering different home elements (roof system, exterior, foundation, rooms, outside structure, etc.) — nothing links or drives anything else.
