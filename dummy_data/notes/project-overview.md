### 5. Sales / Lead Response

#### Current CRM — Golden Eagle Custom System
- Built in-house by Sean Flaherty ~21 years ago. Originally started as a "glorified Rolodex" to replace Act! (which had too few custom fields). Has grown significantly over time.
- Every customer is assigned a **CTM (Customer Tracking Number)** — used across all internal documentation to uniquely identify a customer (handles multiple contacts with the same name).
- Built on a shared MySQL database also used by EagleVision (internal utility site) and the public website — all three are fully integrated.
- Hosted on-premises at Golden Eagle.
- Key features:
  - **Phonetic name lookup**: if a rep doesn't know how to spell a contact's name, the system finds them via phonetic/audio comparison.
  - **Contact form**: phone, email, secondary contact (spouse/partner), home location (build site), status in process, target dates (informational only — do not drive automated processes), and marketing source.
  - **File drawer / department notes**: each department keeps its own notes per customer. Notes are visible across departments. Notes can be tagged as "Golden Eagle eyes only" (hidden from dealers). Notes are **immutable once saved** — cannot be edited or deleted by reps (design decision by a prior owner to maintain an evidentiary log). Only Sean can delete a note (e.g., to remove a duplicate).
  - **Quick info / personal notes**: non-note reminders (e.g., "has a dog," "has children") stored per contact.
  - **Reminder system**: reps can set a future-date reminder to follow up with a contact. In practice, reps largely do not use this feature despite it being available for ~20 years.
  - **Aging report**: shows all open leads by sales stage and days since last activity. Touching a record (adding a note, changing status, etc.) resets the last-modified date and removes the contact from the aging clock. Contacts placed "on hold" are suppressed from the aging report.
- **Lead view / active window (stakeholder input)**: Reps want to see who they are actively working with in a **30-day window** by default, pulled from the CRM. A desired behavior: the active list automatically surfaces anyone from the CRM who hasn't been touched in a defined time period and adds them to the rep's to-do list. The term "disqualified" lead is considered a misnomer — a disqualified lead may simply be a longer-horizon prospect (e.g., building in 7 years) who is still "owned" by the rep and stays in their pipeline. Suggested alternative framing: "nurturing-one" and "nurturing-two" tiers rather than "disqualified."
  - **Contract system**: built into the CRM. Contracts are dynamically generated using a custom **command-tag language** Sean created — tags pull live data from the CRM (names, down payment amount, dates, etc.) and support conditional logic (if/then based on home type). Contract verbiage and line items are editable by staff without code changes. Supports multiple contract types: materials contract, miscellaneous materials contract (post-sale additions), and preliminary proposal.
  - **Management reports**: show counts of leads by status, but are described as weak — do not convey lead quality, interest level, or forecasting utility.
  - **No file/attachment support in notes**: The home-built CRM cannot attach files or documents to customer notes. If a client sends an attachment via email, it is not accessible from within the CRM — reps must go back to their email inbox to locate it. Cross-referencing between CRM notes and email attachments is a named pain point (confirmed by John Wednt).
- **Pain points identified**:
  - No single pipeline view for reps — aging report is the primary management tool.
  - Reminder system goes unused.
  - Statuses are not kept current by reps (example: one recently-retired salesperson had customers who had already moved into homes still listed as "new lead").
  - Notes are scattered and require significant digging to extract relevant context — no "living document" or centralized summary per customer.
  - No cross-departmental project tracking; departments each maintain their own spreadsheets independently.
  - The salesperson effectively acts as a pseudo-project manager, manually coordinating between departments.
  - Management reporting/forecasting is weak and not actionable.
- **Transition concern**: Because the CRM, EagleVision, and website all share a MySQL database, migrating away from the CRM will require careful sequencing. Sean's framing: figure out what breaks, decide which pieces to pull the plug on vs. leave running, and phase the transition deliberately.
