## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Starts each morning reviewing reminders set in the CTM to determine the day's priorities.
- Uses the reminder system more diligently than he believes other reps do — attributes this to his background in production meetings and automated claim management systems.
- Reminders are used for: checking on pricing status, floor plan design status, and follow-ups with leads at various stages.
- After reminders, moves to email — responding to current clients and leads, then documenting email content back into the CTM.
- Documents email content by copying and pasting the full text into the CTM note section. For phone calls, logs a summary (not full content) due to the nature of verbal conversation.
- Strong documentation philosophy: "document, document, document" — everything (calls, emails, texts) should be noted. Comes from insurance/claims background.
- Proactively communicates status updates to clients (e.g., where are we in the floor plan process, what are we waiting for). Views open communication as a core principle.

## Lead Handling & Follow-Up Cadence
- Leads come in via phone (800 number) or email (info@goldeneagle.com). It's first-come-first-served — whoever picks up the phone gets the lead.
- After initial contact and vetting a lead as real, Jeff's follow-up cadence: **1 week → 30 days → 3 months**.
- Determines preferred communication method (phone, text, email) early in the first conversation and uses that method for all follow-ups.
- Outliers exist: some prospects give a specific follow-up timing (e.g., "call me when I close on my property in two weeks") — these override the standard cadence.
- Will disqualify leads that show no meaningful engagement after repeated follow-ups rather than continuing to pester them.
- Each rep has their own individual playbook — no universal enforced sequence across the team.

## CRM / CTM Usage
- All tasks, reminders, and follow-ups are set manually — nothing is automated by the system.
- Copies and pastes text strings (including SMS/text conversations) into the CTM note section, labeling them clearly (e.g., "text string from Jeff and Jim Bob").
- Uses the **Elevate** phone system for SMS texting through office phone numbers.
- Notes personal details about clients (e.g., hobbies, family, what's driving their decision) in a separate area of the CTM designed for personal/quick-info notes. Uses these as rapport hooks in future conversations (e.g., asking about a child's baseball camp).
- Another rep can access his CTM records if he's unavailable (e.g., on vacation), but reps don't work each other's established leads.

## Sales Process
- The primary "first down marker" is getting the prospect to sign a **Design Service Agreement (DSA)** and pay a design deposit.
- Design fee: **$4 per design square foot** for homes up to 10,000 sq ft. Design square footage includes covered decks, porches, garages, finished lower level, etc. — often significantly more than the base footprint. Example: a 2,000 sq ft home may have ~6,700 design sq ft → ~$25,000 deposit.
- This deposit is credited back to the client when they sign the full contract. It functions as a commitment filter — weeds out tire-kickers and protects the engineering/design team's cycle time.
- Once DSA is signed and deposit collected, the engineering department begins creating floor plans and elevations. Client also receives a Google Doc survey to describe how they want to live in the home (interior vision, color palettes, gathering habits, etc.) — feeds the interior design group.
- DocuSign is used for some agreements; upon signing, information is manually entered into the CTM by accounting.
- DSA signed docs/information gets disseminated to departments automatically (via Google Doc) or manually (via accounting for DocuSign).

## Pipeline & Conversion
- Estimated conversion rate from lead to full contract: **~2–3%** (Jeff's words: "if you get one all the way through the pipeline out of 100 leads, you're looking at about 2%, and that'd be conservative").
- Inbound lead volume varies significantly based on social media content drops. On a day when a new home tour drops: **10–20 calls** for that floor plan. On a slow day with no new content: **~5 new lead calls**.

## Relationship-Building Philosophy
- Believes clients "buy me before they buy the product" — personal rapport is critical given the size of the purchase ($600K–millions).
- Uses probing questions to establish personal context: what's driving this decision, where are they looking to build, what are their interests. Uses this information to build a non-transactional relationship.
- Homes are often emotionally significant purchases (forever homes, tied to health decisions, dreams, family milestones).

- Would value a system that tracks non-response patterns and prompts a disqualification decision (e.g., "no response from Jim Bob after 6 attempts — do you want to maintain or disqualify?").
- Described the current manual reminder process as "busy work" — meaningful but time-consuming.
