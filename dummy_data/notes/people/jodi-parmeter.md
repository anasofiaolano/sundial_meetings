# jodi parmeter

**Role:** Sales Rep (new)

## Background
- Has been at Golden Eagle for approximately **3 months**; just started taking sales calls the week of the interview.
- Background is in **interior design**.
- Also owns a **boutique** in town.
- Is the **founder's daughter-in-law** — her husband Rick is one of the brothers, and was a contractor/home builder for 30 years. This gives her practical exposure to home construction even though sales is new to her.
- Was brought onto the sales team by Jay, who believed her interior design background would add a different set of skills — particularly in the upfront design phase of the home, bridging the gap between sales and the selections/design group.
- Notes that her background gives her a different perspective compared to career salespeople: she brings fresh eyes to processes that longer-tenured reps take for granted.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Because she is new, her pipeline consists only of initial contacts entered into the CRM — has not yet progressed leads further than that stage.
- Answers inbound calls along with the rest of the sales team — calls ring through to all sales team phones simultaneously; first person to pick up gets the lead.
- Approximately **half of inbound calls are actual sales calls**; the other half are for Log Home Mart (a division that sells parts/pieces of homes), vendor calls, advertising solicitations, etc. — the team answers everything.
- After an initial call with a prospect, enters their information into the in-house CRM: name, building location, address, email, topics discussed.
- Follows up with a personalized email summarizing the call, pointing to floor plans that match the prospect's wish list, and driving them to the website.
- **Follow-up cadence**: 1 week → follow-up email ("just touching base"); 1 month → phone call; after that → qualify or disqualify the lead.
- Sets reminders manually in the CRM to trigger these follow-ups. If she forgets to set the reminder, nothing prompts her — there is no automation.
- Leads are visible on each rep's home screen and can be tagged as hot, medium, or disqualified.
- A daily checklist/notification pops up showing what tasks are due that day (based on the reminders she has manually set).

## Observations on the Sales Process
- Most inbound callers are coming from **YouTube** — often haven't visited the website before calling, which she finds surprising.
- The job requires deep technical knowledge of home construction (foundations, snow loads, wind rates, engineering specifics) — far beyond a typical sales role. Described this as "daunting" and different from what she expected.
- Described the sales cycle as very long: prospects typically deal with the sales team for roughly **a year and a half on average**. For a very large custom home (e.g., 17,000 sq ft), the relationship can span **2–3 years**.
- The process doesn't end until the client's last load of materials arrives — typically a couple of months before the home is complete.
- Every home is deeply custom — no standard price list, no off-the-shelf floor plan. Clients almost never select an existing plan unchanged.
- Believes deep, educational relationships with prospects are necessary given the custom nature of the product.
- Gets a ballpark price to the client early by estimating based on the floor plan and any modifications (adding bedrooms, bathrooms, etc.), including rough calculations for windows, doors, foundation additions, etc. Tells the client it's within approximately **10% accuracy** — meant to self-qualify/disqualify before investing further.
- After ballpark acceptance, the next step is an **architectural design plan**, priced by the square foot. This is a deposit credited toward the final home price. Covers the cost of the design and engineering teams beginning work.
- Iterates on the floor plan with the client approximately **5–6 times** before moving to a materials proposal (hard price).
- Pricing at the materials proposal stage is extremely detailed — down to individual two-by-fours — handled by a dedicated pricing department.

## Forms / Documentation Pain Points
- There are many separate forms covering different aspects of the home (roof system, exterior, foundation, rooms, outside structure, etc.) — **nothing drives anything else**; they are all standalone.
- Described a concept she's heard discussed internally: a **"living document"** or unified form that links all these elements, flags incompatible product combinations, and auto-populates or removes irrelevant sections based on prior selections.
- Example: if a client chooses no-maintenance siding, the stain/finish pages become irrelevant — but the current forms don't account for this, leaving pages that don't apply.
- Noted that seasoned salespeople barely fill out the existing forms because they know what applies; as a new rep, she reads every line to make sure she isn't missing something. Describes the current forms as potentially "antiquated" — not all sections are valid for current product offerings.
- Described the **Lukas** as having been thinking about the living document concept and noted it would be especially helpful for new salespeople.

## Financial Process Observations
- First deposit (architectural design plan): **minimum $20,000** — must be paid in cash (no financing at this stage).
- Second payment at selections meeting (cabinets, doors, windows, hardware, etc.): another **$20,000**.
- Engineering fee: **$180 for non-stamped plans** or **$280 for stamped plans** (driven by county requirements). This fee is **non-refundable**.
- Total pre-financing outlay before a construction loan kicks in: approximately **$40,000+ in cash**, plus the engineering fee. Jodi noted this is a significant ask — she finds it hard to believe clients walk away at that level of investment, but acknowledged it happens (sometimes due to cash flow issues, stock sales falling through, or misrepresented financial situations).
- Construction loans generally begin to kick in with the **first delivery of materials** — not before.
- Noted awareness of construction loan time limits: if the home isn't complete before the loan's time window closes, borrowers can face holding two mortgages simultaneously.

- Shadowed sales calls, customer meetings, and design kickoff meetings.
