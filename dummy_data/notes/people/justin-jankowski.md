# justin jankowski

**Role:** Client Advisor / Sales

## Background
- Has been at Golden Eagle for **21 years** — first in the design and engineering department, then moved into sales (client advising) about 10 years ago.
- Has also worn other hats over the years, including customer service and retail sales.
- Based in Duluth area (experienced an ice storm during the interview that briefly cut power).

## Company Evolution (Justin's Perspective)
- Has witnessed a major shift in clientele over 21 years: homes were once very small and simplistic with a lot of local sales; now Golden Eagle serves a higher-end, luxury clientele across North America.
- Most of his current clients are in the **$1.5–2.5 million range** for a completely built home.
- Believes there is still room to move further upmarket — vendors Golden Eagle has recently brought in have clientele a step above Golden Eagle's current base.
- Owner Jay wants to capture both smaller projects and high-end clients, which can make it difficult to focus marketing and services. Justin describes this as "a little bit of a struggle."
- YouTube has been a major growth driver — Justin believes hitting the YouTube market is when Golden Eagle "really took it to the next level," enabling reach to clients worldwide, 24/7, 365.
- Trade shows and magazine advertising have been largely abandoned in favor of digital channels.
- Jay's son (Luke) serves as in-house videographer, which Justin notes gives Golden Eagle a cost advantage over third-party video production.
- A newly released home tour video had already reached close to **10,000 views** the morning after it launched — Justin cited this as evidence of the channel's impact.
- Completed client homes with YouTube tours and Matterport walkthroughs function as Golden Eagle's "new models" — replacing the aging physical model home on-site (which is approximately 25 years old).

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Starts each morning checking email — reviews what came in overnight.
- Admits to sometimes checking email on Sunday evenings to see what he's walking into Monday, which causes stress (e.g., an upset client who visited a job site).
- Leads and prospective clients often email over weekends or evenings when they have free time — e.g., after watching YouTube videos.
- Incoming sales leads arrive via a shared **info@goldeneagleloghomes** Outlook inbox; the entire sales team has access and it is **first-come-first-served** — whoever responds first gets the lead.
- Has experienced situations where **two reps responded to the same lead** simultaneously because both grabbed the same email within seconds of each other.
- Considers the current lead distribution system (free-for-all on a shared inbox) inefficient; was unfamiliar with the concept of round-robin lead attribution when it was raised.
- Also receives many interruptions from **non-sales calls** (service calls, vendor calls) coming through the main line — describes this as a persistent complaint.
- Phone rings both at his desk and on his cell phone (via the phone system app), with a delay between the two — sometimes he picks it up on his cell after someone at the office already answered.
- Describes the volume of inbound interruptions across multiple channels as "a lot of distractions."
- Vendors (e.g., shipping companies) frequently call or email the main sales line even after being given direct numbers, which adds to the noise.

## Sales Process & Compensation
- Sales team is **commission-based**; compensation is a percentage of gross materials sales.
- Gets paid when the materials **leave the yard and are invoiced** — not at DSA signing, deposit, or design/engineering contract.
- Can be working with a client for **years** before receiving any commission on that sale.
- Does not earn a spiff on design contracts, deposits, or architectural review fees — only on the first materials delivery.
- Acknowledges this can be frustrating: if a deal falls through after months or years (e.g., a property issue), the rep earns nothing.
- Cited a same-day example: a colleague (John) lost a deal the day of the interview due to a property being designated a wetland — after extensive time invested — with no compensation.

## Deal Fallthrough Rate & Causes
- Approximately **4 clients per year** fail to complete after entering the design/engineering process — described as "very minimal."
- Common reasons for fallthrough:
  1. **Financing** — unable to secure funding.
  2. **Overall cost to build** — after getting the builder's full construction cost, the total exceeds the client's budget.
  3. **HOA restrictions** — not discovered until final plans are submitted; Homeowners Association blocks the build.
  4. **Property issues** — e.g., zoning problems, wetland designations discovered late in the process.
- These causes are largely outside Golden Eagle's control.

## Time Allocation & Bottlenecks
- Spends a significant portion of time in the **design phase** with clients: reviewing designs, processing revisions, selecting materials.
- Design phase involves phone calls, emails, and in-person meetings (e.g., a two-hour in-person meeting with a client who came down from the Upper Peninsula of Michigan).
- Historically, the sales rep was responsible for conveying all client meeting information to the design team via paperwork — described as "very clumsy."
- Process is improving: design and interior design teams are now being included directly in client meetings, reducing the translation layer and expected to reduce revision cycles.
- **Pricing team** still requires physical paper submissions for most revision requests — Justin must walk a printed document to their department basket. Some change order emails are now accepted, but physical paper is still the norm. Justin has raised this as an issue internally.

## Follow-Up / CRM Usage
- Uses the **CRM reminder system** to track warm leads: sets a reminder, which pops up in the CRM, then goes into the client's file to draft a personalized follow-up email.
- Follow-up content includes: references to past conversations, current status questions, and links to new YouTube videos to re-spark interest.
- Cadence after initial contact is based on gut feel from prior conversations — no formally defined sequence.

## AI Familiarity

- Does not copy-paste AI output directly; personalizes the output and removes language he wouldn't naturally use.
- Uses ChatGPT for personal tasks as well.
- Has no strong reservations about AI — describes himself as open to it; just unfamiliar with its full capabilities.
- Interested in learning how AI can help with follow-ups and other sales tasks.
